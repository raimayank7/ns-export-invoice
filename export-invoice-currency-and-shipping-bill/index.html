Currency, port code and shipping bill on an export invoice

The export details section of the maker holds the lines that customs, banks and the e-invoice portal look for. Here is what each one means and where to find it.

The fields

FieldWhat to typeExample
Invoice currencyThe buyer's currency, as a 3-letter codeUSD
Exchange rateRupees for 1 unit of that currency88.50
Country of destinationWhere the goods go (printed with its 2-letter code)United States (US)
Port code6-character customs code of the port, airport or ICD of loadingINNSA1
Shipping bill No. and dateFrom the shipping bill your customs broker files. Often known only after the invoice; add it then and save again.1234567 · 06/11/2026
LUT reference and dateThe ARN of your Letter of Undertaking (only for exports without IGST)AD0304260001234

The value in foreign currency

The maker divides the rupee total by your exchange rate and prints, for example, Invoice value in USD: USD 4,237.29 (at 1 USD = ₹88.50). The rupee figures stay the legal ones for GST. If your buyer agreed a dollar price, work out the rupee rate per unit (dollar price × exchange rate) and type that.

In the e-invoice

For businesses that e-invoice, the export details go into the file's ExpDtls section: shipping bill number (ShipBNo) and date (ShipBDt), port (Port), currency (ForCur), country code (CntCode) and whether you claim a refund (RefClm). The buyer is sent as URP with state and place of supply 96 and PIN 999999, as the schema asks for exports.

Worked example

Invoice total ₹3,75,000 for a buyer in the United States, exchange rate ₹88.50: 375000 ÷ 88.50 = USD 4,237.29. Port INNSA1 (Nhava Sheva), shipping bill filed the next day: open the saved invoice, add the number and date, Save, and print again.

Port codes are customs (ICEGATE) codes; your customs broker or freight forwarder can confirm yours. Exchange rates are notified by CBIC for customs; use the one your accountant advises.
