Export with IGST, or under LUT without IGST?

Exports of goods and services, and supplies to SEZ units and developers, are "zero-rated": no GST should stay in the price. There are two ways to get there, and the invoice shows which one you chose.

Option 1: under LUT or bond, without IGST

  • Before exporting, file a Letter of Undertaking in FORM GST RFD-11 on the GST portal (or a bond). It is usually filed for each financial year.
  • The invoice carries 0% IGST and the "under bond or letter of undertaking without payment of integrated tax" endorsement. Type the LUT reference (ARN) in the maker.
  • You can then claim a refund of the input tax credit you could not use.
  • Time limits (Rule 96A): goods must leave India within 3 months of the invoice date, and for services the payment must come in foreign exchange (or in rupees where the RBI allows) within 1 year. If not, you pay the tax with interest within 15 days after that period, unless the Commissioner allows more time.

Option 2: with IGST paid

  • Charge IGST on the invoice at the normal rate and pay it in your GSTR-3B.
  • For goods, the shipping bill filed with customs works as the refund application. The export invoice details you give in Table 6A of GSTR-1 are matched with the shipping bill, and the IGST refund is paid to your bank account.
  • For services and SEZ supplies, the refund is applied for on the GST portal.

SEZ supplies

A supply to an SEZ unit or developer is treated as an inter-state supply even when the SEZ is in your own state, so it always carries IGST (or 0% under LUT), never CGST and SGST. The SEZ buyer's GSTIN goes on the invoice, and the e-invoice type is SEZWP or SEZWOP.

Which is better?

Under LUTWith IGST
CashNo tax paid up frontTax paid first, refunded later
PaperworkLUT once a year; refund of unused credit if you want itRefund through the shipping bill for goods
RiskTax + interest if goods aren't exported in 3 months or service payment doesn't come in 1 yearRefund held if shipping bill and GSTR-1 don't match

Most small exporters use the LUT. Talk to your accountant before choosing, especially for goods where special refund rules apply.
